The course vision

Learn to think before you type.

Syntax is a tool. The durable skill is turning an unfamiliar situation into a model, an algorithm, evidence, and a better second attempt.

  1. ApproachRestate the goal, work a tiny example, and identify what is known.
  2. ModelChoose the state, operations, constraints, inputs, and desired outputs.
  3. ChooseMatch the structure to a technique: simulation, search, greedy, recursion, or more.
  4. VerifyUse invariants, boundary cases, traces, tests, and explanations—not hope.
  5. ImproveMeasure time, memory, clarity, and generality; then revise deliberately.

Problems evolve

A grid becomes a graph; one robot becomes a team; a working search becomes a shortest-path algorithm. Each project is the next question hiding inside the previous one.

Compare, do not imitate

We keep brute force as a trustworthy baseline, then compare divide-and-conquer, greedy, dynamic programming, backtracking, graph, and randomized strategies.

Make thinking visible

Every major algorithm is traced by hand, animated in the terminal, rendered as ASCII or HTML/SVG, and finally organized as clean modular code.

Modern C++ · our main workshop

C++ makes data representation, performance, value semantics, and abstraction visible. We favor standard-library containers, range-based loops, const-correct interfaces, RAII, tests, and small composable functions.

Rust · a glimpse ahead

Short comparisons show how ownership, borrowing, pattern matching, and strong tooling can prevent whole classes of systems bugs. Different languages encode different trade-offs; choosing one is an engineering decision.

Systems you will know how to build.

Open any build to see the model beneath the spectacle. Each demonstration becomes a project, then a platform for your own extension.

Semester studio

One growing system, every week.

Each milestone includes a working build, a visual explanation, focused challenges, and one open-ended “what if?” exploration.

  1. Week 01

    Command a robot

    Write precise movement instructions, trace state by hand, and ship a tiny terminal robot.

    Decomposition · tests · brute force
  2. Week 02

    Build the grid world

    Model cells, walls, dirt, coordinates, and safe input; render the state as ASCII.

    Types · state · simulation
  3. Week 03

    Make it sweep

    Compare row sweep, wall following, and random walks; define a coverage metric.

    Loops · invariants · randomized algorithms
  4. Week 04

    Discover objects

    Label connected components and animate flood fill one frontier at a time.

    Recursion · DFS · BFS
  5. Week 05

    Find a route

    Turn the grid into a graph, run BFS, reconstruct a shortest path, and test unreachable cases.

    Graph modeling · shortest paths
  6. Week 06

    Move to weighted maps

    Compare BFS, Dijkstra, and a greedy nearest-target policy with visible counterexamples.

    Priority queues · greedy methods
  7. Week 07

    Ship Snake

    Build the timed game, first with sequence state and then constant-time head/tail updates.

    Strings · deques · linked structures
  8. Week 08

    Refactor into objects

    Separate model, renderer, input, and policies; explain and protect each class invariant.

    OOP · interfaces · value semantics
  9. Week 09

    Coordinate a fleet

    Assign cleaning tasks to several agents, detect collisions, and compare fairness strategies.

    Simulation · greedy assignment
  10. Week 10

    Search difficult worlds

    Generate mazes, solve them with backtracking, and use heuristics to prune the search tree.

    Backtracking · search · pruning
  11. Week 11

    Remember useful work

    Optimize repeated subproblems in paths, sequences, or rhythm generation; plot the speedup.

    Dynamic programming · memoization
  12. Week 12

    Divide the problem

    Visualize merge sort or spatial decomposition and compare empirical growth curves.

    Divide and conquer · complexity
  13. Week 13

    Publish a visual explainer

    Replay algorithm events in terminal and HTML/SVG or a modern C++ graphics library.

    Modularity · visualization · documentation
  14. Week 14

    Capstone showcase

    Extend one system, validate it with evidence, teach it live, and submit it to the Hall of Fame.

    Independent learning · synthesis

Lecture 01 · the launch

No “Hello, World” without a world.

The first meeting starts with the end-of-course demos, then students make a robot succeed before learning the C++ needed to express their reasoning.

See the future

Live Snake, robot, route planner, music, and visualization demos. Students predict the hidden state and rules.

Model together

Human teams act as robots on a drawn grid. Ambiguous instructions fail safely and become precise algorithms.

Build in pairs

Translate a tiny sweep into C++, trace every variable, test edge cases, and change one rule.

Explain & reflect

Compare solutions, name an invariant, write the next question, and leave with a runnable artifact.

Course roadmap

From first principles to real programs.

Nine connected modules move from C++ fundamentals to practical systems, with algorithms taught as applications of the structures they operate on.

The instructor’s promise

You will not learn alone.

By the end of this course, you will be able to meet unfamiliar programming problems with the calm, structured confidence of a strong programmer: make a model, choose a strategy, test your reasoning, and improve what you built.

I will be available to help you learn and to work through the obstacles that serious learning inevitably creates. Ask early, bring imperfect attempts, and show your reasoning. Confusion is not a verdict; it is information we can use.

Our practice follows research-backed principles: active recall, worked examples, deliberate practice, timely feedback, collaboration, and repeated opportunities to transfer an idea into a new setting. Every lecture aims to earn your attention by giving you a new way to think—not another page of syntax to memorize.

Your growth matters more than a single grade. Consistent, honest effort, reflection, and revision will be recognized. I cannot promise that the course will always feel easy. I can promise that the difficulty will have a purpose, that your questions will matter, and that you will leave able to learn far beyond this syllabus.

— Dr. Debasish Pattanayak

Learning community

A studio, not a spectator sport.

We build a culture where unfinished thinking is welcome, feedback is specific, and explaining an idea strengthens everyone’s understanding.

Pair & rotate

Driver and navigator roles change often so every student practices implementation and strategic thinking.

  • Explain before editing
  • Rotate partners across backgrounds
  • Credit ideas and assistance

Demo circles

Weekly five-minute showcases normalize partial progress and reveal multiple valid approaches.

  • Show one success and one surprise
  • Ask “what did you try?”
  • Archive useful traces and tests

Help that teaches

Office hours and peer support focus on questions, models, and debugging habits—not copying finished answers.

  • Bring the smallest failing example
  • Use a shared debugging checklist
  • Escalate blockers early

Choice with belonging

Open-ended extensions let interests in games, robotics, art, systems, science, or music shape the same core ideas.

  • Accessible formats by default
  • Low-bandwidth alternatives
  • Community agreements revisited together
